\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Boundary regret connects several literatures that have remained largely separate: human expertise audits, deferral systems, value-of-information theory, and decision-focused learning.

\paragraph{Residual Human Expertise Audits.}
The closest empirical precursor is work that audits whether human predictions contain information not already captured by an algorithm.
\citet{AlurEtAl2023} test for residual expertise using conditional covariance, log-loss improvement, and conditional-independence checks; \citet{AlurRaghavanShah2024} give an algorithmic-indistinguishability account of human expertise in prediction.
These audits ask whether the human has residual predictive information about outcomes, conditional on the model.
Our estimand is different. Boundary regret asks whether the human signal changes the reward-relevant action locally. Residual predictive expertise can therefore exist without residual decision value, and residual decision value can exist without much predictive gain.

\paragraph{Deferral, Selective Prediction, and Reject Options.}
Learning-to-defer methods train a system to choose between acting automatically and handing an instance to a human expert \citep{MozannarSontag2020,HemmerEtAl2023,MaoMohri2023}.
Selective-prediction and reject-option theory study the same kind of choice for classifiers that may abstain instead of issuing an automatic prediction \citep{RamaswamyEtAl2018,FrancPrusa2023}.
These papers optimize triage, abstention, or deferral policies under a specified loss and expert channel.
They decide when to use the human. Boundary regret instead isolates the human information that has local value for a given reward geometry, before imposing any collaboration protocol or learned deferral rule.

\paragraph{Realized Human--AI Collaboration.}
A separate literature asks why deployed human--AI teams often fail to realize complementarity.
\citet{Vaccaro2024} synthesize evidence that human--AI combinations often do not outperform the better standalone agent. \citet{SchoefferEtAl2024} show that explanations can change reliance without necessarily helping users identify correct advice.
\citet{McLaughlinSpiess2024} model recommendation design for realized complementarity, and \citet{HemmerEtAl2025} organize evidence on when complementarity appears in human--AI collaboration.
Our analysis sits before those behavioral and design questions. It measures decision value available in the joint distribution, not whether an interface, incentive scheme, or user response extracts it.

\paragraph{Value of Information and Action-Relevant Geometry.}
Our formal results build on value-of-information theory, originating with Blackwell's theorem that one experiment is more informative than another if and only if it is preferred by every decision-maker \citep{Blackwell1951}.
\citet{RadnerStiglitz1984} show that information value can be locally flat when the optimal action does not change. \citet{DeLaraGossner2020} characterize when the value of a single signal vanishes in a general convex-analytic framework.
\citet{ChenWaggoner2017} study informational substitutes and complements through marginal value and submodularity. In persuasion and Bayes-plausibility models, information value can be written as an expected Jensen gap in posterior value \citep{KamenicaGentzkow2011}.
We specialize this geometry to finite-action reward functions and cross-channel interactions. The resulting substitution is local: it occurs at decision-boundary crossings.
The point is also related to action-set design. \citet{StraitouriEtAl2025} show that narrowing action choices can improve human sequential decisions; boundary regret asks which human information remains valuable for the reward-local action set itself.

\paragraph{Decision-Focused Machine Learning.}
The predict-then-optimize literature makes a parallel observation for optimization pipelines: predictive accuracy and downstream decision quality are distinct objectives \citep{Elmachtoub2022,Wilder2019}.
SPO+ loss trains models to minimize decision regret rather than prediction error.
Our work applies the same principle to human--AI auditing rather than model training: boundary regret measures the decision regret of ignoring the human signal, whereas decision-focused learning endogenizes the reward into the training loss.
The settings are complementary---one concerns model design, the other concerns review allocation---but the underlying insight is shared.
