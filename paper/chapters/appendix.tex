\section{Lean~4 Proof Artifacts}
\label{app:lean}
% ============================================================

All formal results used in the paper are verified in Lean~4 using Mathlib.
The development contains five modules: \texttt{Basics.lean}, \texttt{Regret.lean}, \texttt{JensenGap.lean}, \texttt{FacetActivation.lean}, and the umbrella import \texttt{AllSubstitutionIsLocal.lean}.
Table~\ref{tab:lean-map} maps the paper's claims to Lean declarations.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{Map from paper statements to Lean~4 declarations.}
\label{tab:lean-map}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
Paper & Type & Lean declaration & Module \\
\midrule
$V(b) = \max_a r_a \cdot b$ & Def & \texttt{terminalValue} & Regret \\
$\operatorname{regret}(a,b) \geq 0$ & Lem & \texttt{regret\_nonneg} & Regret \\
$\operatorname{regret}=0 \Leftrightarrow a$ optimal & Lem & \texttt{regret\_eq\_zero\_iff} & Regret \\
$\E[b_{\mathrm{post}}]=b$ & Lem & \texttt{prior\_eq\_weighted\_posterior} & Basics \\
Jensen gap $\geq 0$ & Thm & \texttt{jensenGap\_nonneg} & JensenGap \\
Thm~\ref{thm:voi-br} (VoI $=$ E[BR]) & Thm & \texttt{jensenGap\_eq\_weighted\_regret} & FacetActivation \\
Thm~\ref{thm:facet-bridge} & Thm & \texttt{facet\_activation\_bridge} & FacetActivation \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Primitives.}
\texttt{Basics.lean} defines \texttt{Belief}, \texttt{ObsKernel}, \texttt{marginalProb}, and \texttt{posterior}, and proves the posterior martingale identity \texttt{prior\_eq\_weighted\_posterior}.
\texttt{Regret.lean} defines \texttt{terminalValue} and \texttt{regret} and proves non-negativity and the zero-regret optimality characterization.
\texttt{JensenGap.lean} defines Jensen gaps over posterior lotteries and proves non-negativity for belief-convex functions.

\paragraph{Paper theorems.}
\texttt{FacetActivation.lean} contains the two formal claims used in the text.
The weighted-regret identity \texttt{jensenGap\_eq\_weighted\_regret} rewrites the value of information as expected regret of retaining the prior-optimal action after the posterior update.
The bridge theorem \texttt{facet\_activation\_bridge} states that this expected regret is strictly positive exactly when some reachable posterior strictly prefers a rival action.

\section{CheXpert Label Protocol}
\label{app:exp2-protocol}
% ============================================================

\paragraph{Reader study.}
The five board-certified radiologists (bc1, bc2, bc3, bc5, bc7) provided binary disease-presence ratings under the official CheXpert five-condition protocol (Atelectasis, Cardiomegaly, Consolidation, Edema, Pleural Effusion) on the 500-image frontal-view validation set.
The held-out label $y$ for each reader--task pair is computed by \emph{leave-one-out majority vote}: for GT reader $r_i$, $y = \mathbf{1}[\sum_{j \neq i} v_j \geq 3]$ where $v_j \in \{0,1\}$ are the remaining four GT readers' votes.
This construction ensures $y \perp h$ by design: the test reader is never a voter in the ground truth they are evaluated against.
Benchmark readers (bc4, bc6, bc8) were not voters in the original study, so their pairs use all five GT votes (equivalent to the official majority, no exclusion needed).

\paragraph{Model posterior.}
Logit-space temperature scaling is applied per reader--task pair by minimising NLL on a 30\% held-out calibration split; the calibrated probability is used as $b_x$.

\paragraph{Human-augmented posterior.}
$\bpost_{x,h} \approx P(Y=1\mid x,h)$ is estimated via 5-fold cross-fitted logistic regression on the feature vector $[\operatorname{logit}(b_x),\,h,\,h\cdot\operatorname{logit}(b_x)]$, with folds stratified on $y$.
The LOO label construction ensures structural $h$--$y$ independence; cross-fitting additionally ensures that the augmented-belief estimator and the held-out label are independent within each fold, avoiding estimator overfitting.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
All correlations and boundary-regret estimates are computed on the held-out fold for each instance, then aggregated across folds.
Pairs where $\BRhat\equiv 0$ across the entire evaluation set are excluded from the Spearman $\rho$ computation ($n$ valid).

\section{CIFAR-10H: Theory-Confirming Negative Control}
\label{app:exp2-cifar10h}
% ============================================================

CIFAR-10H evaluates seven pretrained CIFAR-10 classifiers against 50 human annotators across 10 one-vs-rest binary tasks (3{,}500 reader--task pairs).
Pretrained CIFAR-10 classifiers are highly accurate; their softmax probabilities rarely straddle a decision boundary for a given annotator's image subset.
Theorem~\ref{thm:facet-bridge} therefore predicts near-zero actionable boundary-regret mass everywhere --- a prediction the data confirm: only 47.7\% of pairs have any non-zero $\BRhat$ signal at all, and ${\geq}94\%$ of top-disagreement-decile instances show $\BRhat=0$.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{CIFAR-10H negative control: mean Spearman $\bar\rho$ between log-loss gain and $\BRhat$ by reward regime across 3{,}500 pairs (7 models $\times$ 500 annotator--class pairs).}
\label{tab:cifar10h}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
Reward & $\bar\rho$ (valid) & $\bar\rho$ (all) & $N$ valid & Top-decile $\BRhat=0$ \\
\midrule
R1 (thr.\ $0.500$) & $+0.015$ & $+0.007$ & 1{,}668 & $96.2\%$ \\
R2 (thr.\ $0.167$) & $+0.044$ & $+0.024$ & 1{,}905 & $94.5\%$ \\
R3 (thr.\ $0.833$) & $-0.008$ & $-0.004$ & 1{,}632 & $96.9\%$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The R1 dissociation signal ($\bar\rho=+0.015$) is roughly 25$\times$ weaker than the weakest CheXpert model ($+0.392$).
$\BRhat$ allocation correspondingly degenerates to near-random (${\approx}2\%$ win rate vs.\ Margin under R1), confirming that the allocation advantage in Section~\ref{sec:exp3} depends on the model's beliefs straddling reward facets --- a condition that high-accuracy CIFAR-10 classifiers rarely satisfy.
