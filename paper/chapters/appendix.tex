\paragraph{Primitives (\texttt{Basics.lean}, \texttt{Regret.lean}, \texttt{JensenGap.lean}).}
\texttt{Basics.lean} defines \texttt{Belief} (a probability distribution over a finite type), \texttt{ObsKernel} (a likelihood kernel), \texttt{marginalProb}, and \texttt{posterior} via Bayes' rule.
The two foundational identities are \texttt{marginalProb\_sum} ($\sum_o P(o \mid b) = 1$) and \texttt{prior\_eq\_weighted\_posterior} ($b = \sum_o P(o) \cdot b^o$, the Bayesian martingale).
\texttt{Regret.lean} defines \texttt{terminalValue} and \texttt{regret} and proves non-negativity and the optimality characterization.
\texttt{JensenGap.lean} defines the Jensen gap and proves it is non-negative for belief-convex functions, bridging to Mathlib's \texttt{ConvexOn} via the probability simplex.

\paragraph{Paper theorems (\texttt{FacetActivation.lean}, \texttt{InteriorComplementarity.lean}).}
\texttt{FacetActivation.lean} contains the paper's two main results.
The weighted-regret identity (Theorem~\ref{thm:voi-br}) is a direct algebraic manipulation: the Bayesian martingale cancels the prior-level reward, leaving only regret terms at the posteriors.
The facet-activation bridge (Theorem~\ref{thm:facet-bridge}) combines the weighted-regret identity with the rival-action characterization of positive regret (\texttt{regret\_pos\_iff\_rival\_action}).
\texttt{InteriorComplementarity.lean} proves the Bregman decomposition of Jensen gaps ($\text{gap}(g - h) = \text{gap}(g) - \text{gap}(h)$) and the linear-function zero-gap property.

\paragraph{Geometric identities (\texttt{GeometricSubstituteIdentity.lean}, \texttt{GeometricComplementIdentity.lean}).}
These modules develop the quantitative facet-by-facet decomposition of the Jensen gap of $V$, expressing it as a weighted sum of reward-gap norms times perpendicular crossing depths.
\texttt{PiecewiseLinear.lean} establishes the unnormalized posterior value cancellation that makes each summand linear in $b$ for fixed action.
See Appendix~\ref{app:asil} for the mathematical content.

\paragraph{Measure-theoretic extension (\texttt{MeasureTheoreticBelief.lean}).}
This module generalizes the framework to infinite state spaces via Mathlib's \texttt{ProbabilityMeasure}, \texttt{Kernel}, and \texttt{Measure.condKernel}, proving the disintegration theorem and Bayes formula pointwise.
It is included for completeness but not used in the finite-state results of this paper.

\section{All Substitution Is Local: Interaction Identity}
\label{app:asil}
% ============================================================

The boundary-regret results in the main text concern a single human channel.
The broader \textsc{All Substitution Is Local} (ASIL) theory provides a pairwise interaction identity between model and human information channels, explaining when they are complements and when they are substitutes.

\paragraph{Setup.}
Let channel $i$ (model) and channel $j$ (human) each produce posteriors from a common prior $b$.
Define the \emph{complement force} as the Jensen gap of the look-ahead value
\[
  g(b') \;=\; \E_{o_j}[V(\text{post}_j(o_j \mid b'))]
\]
under channel $i$'s posterior lottery, and the \emph{substitute force} as the Jensen gap of the terminal value $V(b')$ under the same lottery.

\paragraph{Interaction identity.}
The change in VoI from observing channel $j$ after channel $i$, versus observing $j$ alone, admits a per-outer-posterior decomposition.
Define twisted rewards $\tilde{r}_{a,o_j}(s) := r_a(s) \cdot k_j(s, o_j)$ for each inner signal $o_j$.
Then:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta-voi-decomp}
  \Delta\VoI(j \mid i, b)
  \;=\;
  \sum_{o_i} P(o_i \mid b, i) \left[
    \sum_{o_j}
      \operatorname{regret}_{\tilde{r}_{o_j}}\!\bigl(a^*,\, \hat{b}_{i,o_i}\bigr)
    \;-\;
    \operatorname{regret}_{r}\!\bigl(a^*,\, \hat{b}_{i,o_i}\bigr)
  \right],
\end{equation}
where $a^*$ is optimal at $b$ and $\hat{b}_{i,o_i}$ is the outer posterior (formalized as \texttt{delta\_voi\_per\_posterior\_identity} in \texttt{GeometricComplementIdentity.lean}).

\paragraph{Geometric decomposition.}
Both forces decompose into facet-specific contributions.
The substitute force equals a weighted sum of reward-gap norms $\|r_{a'} - r_{a^*}\|$ times perpendicular crossing depths $d_\perp$ at the model's decision facets (\texttt{geometric\_substitute\_identity}).
The complement force equals a parallel sum at \emph{twisted} facets induced by the human channel's conditional likelihoods (\texttt{geometric\_complement\_identity}).

\paragraph{Complement/substitute characterization.}
$\Delta\VoI \geq 0$ (complementarity) when the complement force exceeds the substitute force---geometrically, when the model and human cross \emph{different} decision boundaries.
$\Delta\VoI < 0$ (substitutability) when both channels cross the \emph{same} boundary and carry redundant information.
The sign is determined locally, at the level of individual facet crossings.

\begin{theoremA}[Interior Complementarity]\label{thm:interior-compl}
If every reachable outer posterior $\hat{b}_{i,o_i}$ remains in $\mathcal{R}_{a^*}$ (no facet crossing), then the substitute force vanishes and $\Delta\VoI(j \mid i, b) \geq 0$.
\end{theoremA}

\begin{theoremA}[Substitution Requires Boundary Crossing]\label{thm:sub-requires-crossing}
If $\Delta\VoI(j \mid i, b) < 0$ (channels are substitutes) and the complement force is non-negative, then some outer posterior lies outside $\mathcal{R}_{a^*}$: channel $i$ crosses a decision facet.
\end{theoremA}

\noindent
Experiment~1, Conditions~C4 and~C5, verify these predictions: same-boundary channels are substitutes ($\Delta\VoI < 0$ in all configurations), different-boundary channels are complements ($\Delta\VoI > 0$ for $|\mathcal{A}| \geq 3$; $\Delta\VoI \leq 0$ for $|\mathcal{A}| = 2$, where only one boundary exists).

\paragraph{Relation to the main text.}
Corollary~\ref{cor:zero-dv} is the human-AI specialization of Theorem~\ref{thm:interior-compl} (Interior Complementarity): set $k_i$ to the model channel and ask only whether the model's posteriors remain inside $\mathcal{R}_{a_x}$.
The main-text analysis drops $k_j$ from the interaction and retains only the boundary-regret quantity $\BR(x) = \E_h[\BR(x,h)]$, which is the substitute force evaluated at the model's posteriors.
The ASIL identity retains both channels and characterizes when acquiring the human signal adds value beyond what the model already provides.
