\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

The experiments are sequential.
Section~\ref{sec:exp1} validates the boundary-regret estimator in synthetic settings where the ground truth is computable in closed form.
Section~\ref{sec:exp2} tests whether residual predictive expertise and residual decision value diverge on a real human--AI dataset across reward structures.
Section~\ref{sec:exp3} asks whether an ex ante boundary-regret score can allocate scarce human review more efficiently than standard baselines under asymmetric rewards, validated on both synthetic and real data.

\subsection{Experiment 1: Estimator and Geometry Validation}
\label{sec:exp1}

\paragraph{Question.}
Can the plug-in estimator recover known boundary regret in finite Bayesian decision problems, and avoid mistaking predictive gain for decision value?

\paragraph{Setup.}
We simulate finite-state Bayesian decision problems with known prior, reward matrix, model channel $P(m\mid y)$, and human channel $P(h\mid y)$, with $M \perp H \mid Y$.
For each sampled triple $(y,m,h)$ we compute the exact posterior after the model signal, $b_m=P(y\mid m)$, the posterior after both signals, $b_{m,h}=P(y\mid m,h)$, the model-only action $a_m$, and true instance-level boundary regret
\[
  \BR(m,h) \;=\; V(b_{m,h}) - r_{a_m}\cdot b_{m,h}.
\]
The estimator does not see the true channels: it estimates $\hat P(m\mid y)$, $\hat P(h\mid y)$, and $\hat p(y)$ from finite data using 5-fold cross-fitting and Laplace-smoothed counts, then computes the plug-in $\BRhat$ on held-out instances.
Each condition uses $N=10{,}000$ samples.

\paragraph{Conditions.}
The configuration grid is derived from two axes: $G\in\{2,3,5\}$ decision groups and $K\in\{G,2G\}$ latent states.
Thus $G=2$ is the minimal boundary case, $G=3$ is the smallest genuinely multi-action case, $G=5$ is a moderate stress test, and $K=2G$ checks that the result is not specific to one state per action.
We use three condition roles.
C1 is a positive control: actions reward state groups and the human signal crosses decision boundaries.
C2 is a weak null: the prior is deep inside one decision region and the human channel is weak.
C3 is a strong null: the human channel is highly predictive, but a safe action remains optimal for every reachable posterior.
In C3 the safe-action threshold is not hand-picked: for each configuration we compute the maximum reachable posterior group mass $U$ under the exact channels and set the threshold $\tau=(1+U)/2$.

\paragraph{Results.}
Table~\ref{tab:exp1-core} reports metrics averaged across the six configurations.
For nonconstant true $\BR$ we report Spearman rank correlation; for the null conditions, the relevant metric is false-positive rate.

The estimator recovers the intended geometry.
In C1, where boundary crossings occur on 37.9\% of sampled instances, $\BRhat$ ranks true boundary regret well ($\rho=0.901$); per-configuration detail is in Appendix~\ref{app:exp1-detail}.
In both nulls, the estimator has zero false positives.
C3 is the key diagnostic: the human signal is informative about exactly the feature the reward depends on (group membership), yet boundary regret is zero in every reachable signal pair because no posterior crosses the certified safe-action threshold.
Predictive gain on \emph{task-relevant} signal does not imply decision value when a safe action dominates the reachable belief region; $\BRhat$ inherits the calibration of the input posteriors, so recalibration should precede deployment.

\subsection{Experiment 2: Residual Expertise Does Not Equal Decision Value}
\label{sec:exp2}

\paragraph{Question.}
Does residual predictive expertise track residual decision value, or do the two estimands dissociate as a function of reward geometry --- as Theorem~\ref{thm:facet-bridge} predicts?

\paragraph{Setup.}
We pair a pretrained DenseNet-121 chest X-ray classifier \citep{Huang2017,Cohen2022} with the five board-certified radiologists of the CheXpert reader study \citep{Irvin2019}, evaluated across the five official CheXpert competition conditions (Atelectasis, Cardiomegaly, Consolidation, Edema, Pleural Effusion) on 500 frontal radiographs, yielding $N=25$ reader--task pairs.
The held-out label is the majority vote of these five readers' annotations; full protocol details are in Appendix~\ref{app:exp2-protocol}.
Model beliefs are temperature-scaled per pair, and the human-augmented posterior $\bpost_{x,h}\approx P(Y=1\mid x,h)$ is estimated via 5-fold cross-fitted logistic regression on $[\operatorname{logit}(b_x),\,h,\,h{\cdot}\operatorname{logit}(b_x)]$, with folds stratified on $y$.
Spearman correlations and bootstrap confidence intervals are computed over the 25 pairs.

\paragraph{Reward regimes.}
We evaluate three reward matrices $r_{a,y}$.
R1 is the identity ($+1$ for correct decisions, $0$ for errors), giving threshold $0.5$.
R2 ($r_{0,0}=5$, $r_{1,1}=1$, errors $0$) penalises false positives five times as heavily as false negatives, shifting the threshold to $5/6\approx 0.833$.
R3 mirrors R2 with the asymmetry reversed ($r_{0,0}=1$, $r_{1,1}=5$), threshold $1/6\approx 0.167$.
The 5:1 asymmetry is clinically grounded via the threshold-probability framework of decision curve analysis \citep{Vickers2006}: R3 ($p_t=1/6$) corresponds to a screening-flag threshold and R2 ($p_t=5/6$) to a high-cost-of-intervention threshold, together bracketing the range commonly evaluated in clinical decision analysis for chest pathology.

\paragraph{Reward-Conditional Dissociation.}
The Spearman correlation between residual predictive expertise (log-loss gain over the model alone) and boundary regret inverts across reward regimes: $+0.477$ under R1 (all 25 pairs positive), $-0.112$ under R2 (all 7 valid pairs negative; the remaining 18 have $\BRhat\equiv 0$ across the entire R2 evaluation set --- no posterior update reaches the false-positive-penalty facet), and $-0.051$ under R3 (15 of 25 pairs positive).
Same model, same readers, same conditions: only the reward changes.
Under R2, $97.4\%$ of top-expertise-decile cases --- those where the model is most surprised by the reader's answer --- carry zero boundary regret: the model belief sits far from the false-positive-penalty facet and no single-reader update changes the deployed action.
Under R1, the threshold $0.50$ sits in a dense region of model beliefs, so radiologist readings frequently push the posterior across a facet and predictive expertise aligns with decision value.
Under R3 (threshold $0.167$), dissociation is intermediate ($28.3\%$ top-decile zero-$\BRhat$): the boundary sits closer to the tail than R1 but closer to the bulk than R2.
This is \emph{reward-conditional dissociation}: a direct consequence of reward geometry, not a calibration failure or model breakdown.
Facet distance correlates negatively with $\BRhat$ under every reward ($-0.599$, $-0.221$, $-0.866$ for R1, R2, R3) --- direct empirical confirmation of Theorem~\ref{thm:facet-bridge}: boundary regret concentrates near the active decision facet, independent of how predictive the reader is.

\paragraph{Implication for Predictive Audits.}
A residual-expertise audit in the style of \citet{AlurEtAl2023,AlurRaghavanShah2024} would find substantial predictive value here --- mean log-loss gain is $0.387$ nats across all 25 pairs --- and recommend deploying the readers.
Under R2, $97.4\%$ of those same cases carry zero decision value.
The \emph{estimand shift} from predictive to decisional complementarity is not a refinement; it changes the sign of the deployment recommendation.
If this regime-conditional inversion is theorem-predicted, an ex ante $\BRhat$ score should inherit it: dominating reward-aware geometric proximity where boundary-regret mass is reachable, converging where it is not.
Section~\ref{sec:exp3} tests this directly.
Appendix~\ref{app:robustness} extends this analysis to all six DenseNet checkpoints and all eight readers (240 reader--task pairs); the direction of the dissociation is preserved.

\subsection{Experiment 3: Review Allocation under Scarce Human Input}
\label{sec:exp3}

\paragraph{Question.}
Section~\ref{sec:exp2} established that residual predictive expertise and boundary regret invert across reward regimes, exactly as Theorem~\ref{thm:facet-bridge} predicts.
We now test whether this inversion is actionable: an ex ante $\BRhat$ score should dominate reward-aware geometric proximity where boundary-regret mass is reachable and converge with it where it is not.


\paragraph{Setup.}
We use the same 25 reader--task pairs as in Section~\ref{sec:exp2}.
For each review budget $q\in\{5\%,\,10\%,\,20\%,\,50\%\}$, a policy ranks cases using only pre-review ($x$-available) scores; reviewed cases take the human-updated action $\argmax_a r_a\cdot\bpost_{x,h}$, while unreviewed cases retain $a_x$.
Utility is evaluated on held-out labels under each reward matrix, with policy scores 5-fold cross-fitted on the same fold structure used to estimate $\bpost_{x,h}$.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
We compare $\BRhat$ against six baselines.
\textbf{Margin} (reward-aware distance to the active decision facet) is the primary comparison baseline: it captures reward geometry without any estimate of the human signal $P(h\mid x)$.
\textbf{Entropy} is reward-blind model uncertainty.
\textbf{Residual} is a residual-expertise score --- the deployable analogue of a predictive audit in the style of \citet{AlurEtAl2023}.
\textbf{L2D} is a cross-fitted learning-to-defer classifier \citep{MozannarSontag2020,MaoMohri2023}.
\textbf{Random} is the uninformed baseline.
\textbf{Oracle} is the hindsight upper bound using realized labels.

\paragraph{Regime-conditional inversion.}
Three reward matrices, one model, twenty-five reader--task pairs.
The same scoring rule produces a regime-conditional inversion of allocation advantage: $\BRhat$ dominates Margin under R1 at every budget (sign-test $p<0.0001$), converges to Margin under R2 (only 7 of 25 pairs carry non-zero mass), and is indistinguishable from Margin under R3 (sparse actionable mass at threshold $0.167$).
The pattern across rewards is the result; the wins are the evidence.

\paragraph{R1 (symmetric reward, threshold $0.500$).}
$\BRhat$ wins decisively at every budget (sign-test $p<0.0001$ throughout).
The four budget tallies $21/1/3$, $25/0/0$, $25/0/0$, $25/0/0$ yield $95\%$, $100\%$, $100\%$, $100\%$ win rates.
At $q=10\%$ through $q=50\%$, $\BRhat$ wins all 25 pairs.
At $q=5\%$, 3 ties are pairs where both policies select identical case sets; on the 22 decisive pairs, $\BRhat$ wins 21 ($95\%$ decisive-win rate).

\paragraph{R2 (FP-penalized reward, threshold $0.833$).}
The reward boundary sits at $5/6\approx 0.833$, in the low-density tail of model beliefs.
Eighteen of twenty-five pairs have $\BRhat\equiv 0$ across the entire evaluation set (no reachable boundary crossing under R2's reward); among the 7 pairs with non-zero mass, $\BRhat$ ties Margin exactly at every budget ($q=20\%$: both $+0.0029$, W/L/T $=0/0/25$).
The geometry already isolates the only feasible boundary crossings; this is Theorem~\ref{thm:facet-bridge} confirmed, not contradicted.
The full R2 table is in Appendix~\ref{app:exp3-r2}.

\paragraph{R3 (FN-penalized reward, threshold $0.167$).}
The reward boundary at $1/6\approx 0.167$ sits closer to the belief mode than R2 but further than R1.
$\BRhat$ is indistinguishable from Margin at all tested budgets ($3/0/22$ to $3/1/21$; sign-test $p\geq 0.25$).
The near-null result reflects sparse actionable mass at threshold $0.167$: with LOO-corrected labels, the posterior updates that cross the R3 facet are too infrequent to produce a reliable allocation advantage over reward-aware Margin.

\paragraph{Human channel versus geometric proximity.}
Reward-aware distance to the active facet is a strong baseline: it already identifies which cases are near the decision boundary.
The $95$--$100\%$ paired wins over Margin under R1 (sign-test $p<0.0001$ at every budget) show that $\BRhat$'s estimate of $P(h\mid x)$ and $P(Y\mid x,h)$ adds reliable signal above that geometric distance where actionable mass is dense.
Where mass is absent (R2) or sparse (R3), $\BRhat$ and Margin converge --- both outcomes predicted by Theorem~\ref{thm:facet-bridge}.
A broader negative control extends this: on CIFAR-10H, highly accurate classifiers rarely straddle any reward facet, and $\BRhat$ allocation degenerates to near-random across all rewards and budgets (Appendix~\ref{app:exp3-cifar10h}).
