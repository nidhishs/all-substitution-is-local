\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

In batch active learning the learner commits to a pool of queries before observing any labels.
Two queries are \emph{redundant} when one answers the question the other would have answered; labeling both adds nothing.
Query-by-Committee \citep{SeungOS1992} avoids this by diversifying committee disagreement; BALD \citep{Houlsby2011} by mutual information; BatchBALD \citep{Kirsch2019} and BADGE \citep{Ash2020} by gradient diversity.
Each targets a different proxy for the same question: when are two queries substitutes?
None gives a formal answer.

We characterize the answer geometrically: two queries substitute or complement depending on where the posteriors fall relative to the current decision boundary. Writing the pointwise interaction as $\Delta\VoI(j \mid i, b)$ (the change in channel~$j$'s value of information after observing channel~$i$ at belief~$b$) following \citet{ChenWaggoner2017}, we prove three results:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Bregman decomposition} (Proposition~\ref{prop:decomposition}): $\Delta\VoI(j \mid i, b) = \E[D_g(\bpost, b)] - \E[D_h(\bpost, b)]$, where $D_g$ and $D_h$ are Bregman divergences of auxiliary convex functions. The complement force $\E[D_g]$ measures how much channel~$i$ sharpens channel~$j$'s value of information; the substitute force $\E[D_h]$ measures how much it resolves the current decision.
  \item \textbf{Interior complementarity} (Theorem~\ref{thm:interior}): When all posteriors of channel~$i$ stay within the current decision region, the substitute force is zero and $\Delta\VoI(j \mid i, b) \geq 0$: the query complements everything else in the batch.
  \item \textbf{Converse} (Theorem~\ref{thm:converse}): $\Delta\VoI(j \mid i, b) < 0$ only if at least one posterior of channel~$i$ crosses into a different decision region.
\end{enumerate}
The three results connect to active learning through a pointwise equivalence: $\VoI(i \mid b) > 0$ iff some posterior of channel~$i$ prefers a rival action over the prior-optimal one, a PWLC-computable specialization of \citet{DeLaraGossner2020}'s general-convex characterization.
These yield an exact criterion for batch redundancy: two queries substitute iff at least one admits a posterior that changes the predicted label, and complement otherwise. Crossing a boundary is necessary for substitution but not sufficient, since channels can cross and still complement each other.

The PWLC structure of the value function comes from having finitely many actions, not from any assumption on the model class, so the results apply to any Bayesian classifier.
QBC disagreement, BatchBALD diversity, and BADGE gradient diversity all approximate this criterion on finite posterior draws; we state it exactly and prove it.
