\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced boundary regret as the deployment estimand for human--AI complementarity, shifting the audit from ``does the human have residual predictive information?'' to ``does that information change the reward-relevant action?''
The formal characterization---VoI equals expected boundary regret, with a Lean-verified geometric bridge---grounds this shift by making decision value local to the reward boundary induced by the deployed action set.
On CheXpert across 25 reader--task pairs, residual expertise and decision value diverge in a reward-dependent manner precisely as the theory predicts: $96.0\%$ of top-expertise-decile cases carry zero decision value under a false-positive-penalty reward, and an ex ante boundary-regret score allocates review more efficiently than reward-aware and reward-agnostic baselines where boundary-regret mass is reachable, converging to reward-aware margin where it is not.

\paragraph{Limitations and future work.}
Both Section~\ref{sec:exp3} evaluations are retrospective; prospective evaluation with operational review protocols is required before deployment claims, and the L2D baseline is trained on accuracy not reward-specific utility, disadvantaging it under asymmetric rewards. Section~\ref{sec:exp2} covers a single dataset (CheXpert) and modality; generalization to other domains and human-signal types remains open. Boundary regret measures decision value available in $(x, h, y)$, not whether a deployed workflow can extract it---behavioral frictions \citep{Vaccaro2024,BucinaEtAl2021} require prospective studies. Two natural extensions are integration with learning-to-defer, using boundary regret as an interpretable diagnostic for what learned deferral policies exploit, and prospective studies that test whether interfaces can elicit and use the decision-relevant human signal.
