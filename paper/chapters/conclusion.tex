\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The interaction between information channels respects a localization principle: complement forces are diffuse across the belief interior, but substitute forces concentrate at decision boundaries.
A channel whose posteriors remain interior to the current decision region complements everything else in the batch, regardless of channel correlation, even when the channel itself cannot change the decision.
Substitution is possible only when at least one posterior crosses into a different region, though crossing is necessary, not sufficient.
These results hold for any Bayesian classifier with a finite action space and are formalized in Lean~4.

\paragraph{Applications.}
With $K$ class labels and a finite classifier family, the piecewise-linear value structure is exactly the one batch active learning induces.
QBC \citep{SeungOS1992}, BALD \citep{Houlsby2011}, BatchBALD \citep{Kirsch2019}, and BADGE \citep{Ash2020} each approximate the rival-action criterion we characterize; the criterion specializes \citet{DeLaraGossner2020}'s general characterization to piecewise-linear value functions and makes the cross-channel interaction of \citet{ChenWaggoner2017} pointwise-testable.
The same geometry arises in Bayesian optimization over discrete candidate sets: two evaluations are non-redundant unless at least one admits a posterior that flips the current best candidate.
Sensor placement for finite-hypothesis classification, sequential experimental design over discrete arms, and policy evaluation across finitely many treatments all inherit the decomposition.
Batch active learning is the expository setting; the criterion is not.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The results are specific to finite-action settings where the value function is piecewise-linear: continuous action spaces yield a smooth, strictly-convex $V$ with no flat decision regions, so Theorem~\ref{thm:interior} has no direct analogue.
Replacing decision regions with level sets of the policy map is one extension.
The decomposition requires the Bayesian martingale property $\E[\bpost] = b$; agents with miscalibrated updates break this identity, converting the geometric criterion into an approximation problem.
The two-forces interpretation of Proposition~\ref{prop:decomposition} assumes conditional independence between channels; the decomposition identity and Theorems~\ref{thm:interior}--\ref{thm:converse} hold without this assumption.

\paragraph{Open Questions.}
A gap remains between Theorem~\ref{thm:interior}'s sufficient condition and Theorem~\ref{thm:converse}'s necessary condition: boundary-crossing channels can still complement when the complementary force dominates, and characterizing this crossover is open.
The interaction is generically asymmetric: channel $i$ may substitute for $j$ while $j$ complements $i$, and this asymmetry has consequences for optimal ordering in sequential acquisition.
The criterion concerns exact posteriors; practical pipelines approximate these with sampled beliefs from ensembles, MC-dropout, or last-layer Bayesian networks, and bounding the approximation error remains open.
The geometry, however, is exact: wherever finitely many actions meet a probabilistic world, substitution is concentrated along the thin boundaries where decisions change, and everywhere else, information cooperates.
