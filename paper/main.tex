\begin{abstract}
A human with residual predictive expertise beyond the model may still have zero decision value.
Whether the human's information crosses a reward-relevant boundary---not whether it improves predictions---determines whether review is worth deploying.
We introduce \emph{boundary regret}: the regret of keeping the model's action under the human-updated posterior.
In any finite-action Bayesian decision problem, the value of human review equals expected boundary regret (Lean-formalized); predictive signal that does not cross a reward-induced facet has zero decision value, regardless of log-loss gain.
On CheXpert Cardiomegaly with a board-certified radiologist as the human signal, \textbf{97\%} of top-expertise-decile cases have zero boundary regret under a false-positive-penalty reward: the human is informative, the operative action is unchanged.
On synthetic allocation tasks, an ex ante boundary-regret estimator allocates scarce review better than uncertainty, residual-expertise, and learning-to-defer baselines under asymmetric rewards at review budgets $q \in \{10\%,\, 20\%\}$, with up to $2.7\times$ higher utility gain per review than the best non-oracle alternative.
The deployment estimand for human--AI complementarity is residual decision value, not residual predictive expertise.
\end{abstract}
